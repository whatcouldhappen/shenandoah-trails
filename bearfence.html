Bearfence Mountain Trail

Bearfence is a short ridge hike where the terrain matters more than the mileage. The blue-blazed loop climbs over exposed rock to a summit with views east across the Virginia Piedmont and west into the Shenandoah Valley.

What to expect on the hike

Hikers like the combination of hands-on scrambling and a 360-degree view. The short mileage leaves plenty of time to pause on the ridge.

The steep rock moves are the demanding part. Reviewers recommend shoes with good grip and taking the scramble at your own pace, especially when other hikers are waiting.

Based on a reader-supplied summary of AllTrails hiker reviews.

Prefer to avoid cliff edges? Choose the Bearfence Viewpoint route from the same parking area. The embedded AllTrails route crosses the exposed scramble. The park advises against the scramble for hikers uncomfortable with heights and when the rock is wet or icy.

From Skyland to the ridge

Drive south on Skyline Drive to the Bearfence parking area at mile 56.4, about 15 road miles from Skyland’s north entrance. Cross Skyline Drive and climb the steps. The trail post at the top is where the two hikes diverge.

For the scramble, continue straight on Bearfence Loop Trail. Follow the blue blazes as the path gives way to rock and you use your hands to climb. Beyond the summit, there is more scrambling before you can settle back into a walking rhythm. The loop returns by the Appalachian Trail.

The park’s published scramble hike is listed separately at 1.4 miles, 311 feet of gain and about an hour. Use the AllTrails map below for the specific loop shared here.

Bearfence Viewpoint without the scramble

At the post above the steps, turn right onto the southbound Appalachian Trail. Take the connector on the left, then turn right on Bearfence Loop Trail to reach a viewpoint over the valley. Retrace your steps from there. The park estimates 45 minutes for this 1.1-mile out-and-back, with 305 feet of climbing and a 180-degree view. View the park’s viewpoint hike.
