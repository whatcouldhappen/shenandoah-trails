Hightop Peak Trail

The Appalachian Trail winds uphill through the woods to a short spur overlooking the valley to the west. Hightop makes a good half-day outing if you enjoy a sustained climb: the rocky viewpoint comes at the end of the ascent, and you can decide how far onto it you want to go.

What to expect on the hike

Hikers liked the well-kept woodland path and the view at the end of the climb. The ascent is steady, gaining about 935 feet on the round trip.

Reviewers mention occasional crowds and ticks. They suggest an early start for a quieter walk and a tick check afterward.

Based on a reader-supplied summary of AllTrails hiker reviews.

Getting there from Skyland

Drive south on Skyline Drive to the Hightop Mountain parking area at mile 66.7, about 25 road miles from Skyland’s north entrance. Cross Skyline Drive at the trailhead to pick up the white-blazed Appalachian Trail.

Up the ridge to the viewpoint

Follow the white blazes as the trail bends up the ridge. On a May hike, look along the forest floor for trillium and star chickweed. Near the top, the Appalachian Trail curves left around the summit. Watch on the right for the short side trail: that turn is what brings you to the west-facing view.

The viewpoint is on open rock. If you prefer to avoid edges, pause where the footing feels comfortable. Retrace the spur, then descend the same Appalachian Trail to the road crossing.
